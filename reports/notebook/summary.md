# Loan portfolio · pre-modeling EDA
*Target analysed: addNegStatus (default - change it with --target)*

## Five things to know

1. **The task.** Predict `addNegStatus`: 3,558 of 100,000 loans are positive (3.56%, about 1 in 28). It is a rare-event problem: judge models with ROC-AUC and PR-AUC, never accuracy. What the label exactly means is not documented - ask the organisers. The 10 files behave like random chunks of one population, so pool them.
2. **Do not use as inputs: the delinquency columns and the supplied score.** 12 of the 14 delinquency / penalty columns reach AUC ≥ 0.9 on their own (together 0.986). They record the arrears state the label itself reflects: a small rule over them rebuilds 71% of the events at 93% precision. The supplied score / PD is driven by the same information: AUC 0.93 overall, but 0.59 (±0.07) on the 63,073 loans with no delinquency history (70 events).
3. **Treat high scores with suspicion.** Columns known when the loan is granted already reach AUC 0.969; a good application scorecard usually lands between 0.65 and 0.80. Even without the 4 of them that follow the loan's arrears state (INTEREST_RATE_NOMINAL, ECONOMIC_SECTORS, ECONOMIC_SECTORS_PURPOSE, PRINCIPAL_PAYMENT_FREQUENCY) the AUC is 0.947. 14 of 17 relationships the dictionary promises break in more than 5% of rows in a way a statistical generator reproduces, so the data may be synthetic or heavily perturbed. A high score here does not prove a good model.
4. **Start here.** Load a column set from `reports/feature_roles.csv`: A = known at origination (22 columns, AUC 0.969); B = + balances and schedule (38 columns, AUC 0.991); C = + delinquency columns (52 columns, AUC 0.994). Validate with stratified 5-fold CV and keep `df_10` sealed for the final test. Pick the framing (section 10) before the algorithm.
5. **Before you invest time, ask the organisers** the 11 questions at the end of section 10 - first the metric and submission format, then whether the delinquency columns and the score may be used as inputs.

## More findings

- **Can the data be trusted?** Structurally yes (0 missing cells, 0 duplicate rows). But 14 of 17 dictionary relationships fail in more than 5% of rows (worst: `monthly loans: ContractTermMonth = standardInstalmentCount (+-1)`, 57%). A model that knows only each column's distribution and the rank correlations predicts the violation rates within 1.9 percentage points (independent columns: 12.0), which is what a tabular generator - or heavy per-column noise - would produce; real extracts also break some identities for mundane reasons.
- **One cut-off already tells a lot.** `TotalNumberDelinquencyDays` > 1,253 flags 3,236 loans with precision 85% and recall 78% (Jaccard 0.69). The label is a loan state, not a single threshold.
- **Delinquency histories repeat the same few values.** 10 exact values (away from any column ceiling) catch 71% of all events at 75% precision (picked on these same rows).
- **A result to double-check.** Without delinquency columns and without the supplied score a boosted-tree model still reaches AUC 0.991; with columns known at origination only, 0.969. On the same 25 columns and folds, logistic regression gets 0.959 against 0.972 for boosted trees.
- **How to validate.** A different random seed moves the AUC by 0.0003, so smaller differences between models are noise. Leaving whole files out changes it by -0.0002, so ordinary stratified CV is fine. Class weights change the AUC by -0.0004 and the Brier score from 0.0087 to 0.0174: start without re-weighting.
- **A stress test without dates.** Training only on young loans and testing on old ones (6,561 loans) costs 0.016 AUC for known at origination (set A); 0.004 AUC for no delinquency columns, no supplied score: the signal survives a change of vintage.
- **Sanity checks that fail.** The borrower profile alone (customer + income columns) reaches AUC 0.824, and five columns with little direct link to default (age, income, loan amount, interest rate, term) alone reach 0.865; such columns normally stay near 0.6-0.7. Income and rate carry some genuine risk signal, so this is supporting evidence, not proof, that the label and the columns were generated together.
- **No single leak to remove.** Even after deleting the 5 strongest application-time columns (ECONOMIC_SECTORS_PURPOSE, ECONOMIC_SECTORS, ContractTermMonth …) a re-trained model keeps AUC 0.936: the signal is shared by many columns.
- **A handful of yes/no markers explain most of it.** Counting simple markers (last6Month=0, ECONOMIC_SECTORS=0 without business, standardInstalmentAmount=0, OutstandingPrincipalAm=0 …) - no model at all - gives AUC 0.926 out-of-fold: 93% of events carry at least one marker versus 22% of non-events, and the event rate goes from 0.3% (no marker) to 94% (5 markers).
- **The 'run-off' signature.** Count how many of the 8 balance / schedule columns are exactly 0 (a closed, written-off or fully-due loan has several): the event rate goes from 1.2% (none) to 68% (5 of them), and this one count has AUC 0.812.
- **Events are concentrated.** The 6 riskiest cohorts over PRINCIPAL_PAYMENT_FREQUENCY, PRODUCT_PURPOSE, loanType … hold 6.1% of the loans but 51% of all events.
- **Where the signal lives.** With delinquency and score removed, the strongest single family is 'Instalment schedule' (AUC 0.97 alone); the build-up curve reaches 0.989 after 6 families.
- **Groups.** Sensitive attributes are in the data and predictive. The application-time model flags `SEX = 2` at 0.47x the average rate, less often than average; that group's event rate is 0.43x the average (688 events). See the group audit in section 9.
- **Mock & synthetic bank data (sections 11-14).** 50,000 simulated customers are exact rows of the supplied loans, with the supplied label (3.64% positive). Through the bank APIs a lender would see the loan itself for only 30% of them. The simulated behaviour is generated from a latent stress score that is a formula of the PD rank, the delinquency days and the label itself (R² 1.0000), so AUCs computed from it (bank behaviour alone 0.96, with the loan columns 0.99) measure planted signal, not whether open-banking data predicts default. Use it to build and test pipelines and feature code (a salary-based income estimate lands within ±10% for 94% of salaried customers), not to estimate predictive value.

## Findings that need attention

- **CRITICAL** - 12 of 14 delinquency / penalty columns are fingerprints of the label (single-column AUC ≥ 0.9). median single-column AUC 0.92, max 0.96; the weakest are `ShortDaysInDeliquency` (0.59), `penaltyPaid` (0.67). The dictionary does not define the status, but the data show it travels with heavy arrears recorded at the same snapshot (overdue amounts, days past due, penalties). -> In a prediction track, exclude them: a model that uses them detects the status instead of predicting it. Use them only to understand the label, or in a status-detection / triage track and only if the organisers confirm they exist when you must score.
- **CRITICAL** - The simulated bank behaviour is generated from a score that contains the label. Spending, balances, lateness, micro-loans and gambling in the mock are driven by a latent stress that is a formula of the supplied PD, the delinquency days and `addNegStatus` itself (R² 1.0000). Any model that predicts the label from these transactions is partly reading the label back out of a function of it, and the PD and delinquency columns are exactly the ones section 8 says must not be inputs. The AUCs in section 14 therefore measure how much planted signal survives the bank views, never whether open-banking data predicts default. -> use the mock to build and test pipelines and feature code; do not report its AUC as evidence about real data
- **WARNING** - Application-time columns alone reach AUC 0.97 - unusually high for credit risk. Application scorecards typically sit between 0.65 and 0.80 (behavioural models that see arrears can reach 0.90). With balances, schedule, loan events and bureau snapshot columns removed the model still separates the classes almost perfectly (± 0.004); something in the 'safe' columns still encodes the outcome. -> Treat results above ~0.85 as suspect: look at which columns drive them (below) and ask the organisers how the label and the columns were produced.
- **WARNING** - Columns do not obey their own definitions. 14 of 17 dictionary relationships fail in more than 5% of rows (worst: monthly loans: ContractTermMonth = standardInstalmentCount (+-1), 56.8%). Arithmetic between columns cannot be trusted (see the generator test below for what this pattern means). -> Keep the supplied ratios (PTI, LTV) as they are and do not rebuild them from components; validate every engineered ratio on its own.
- **WARNING** - The broken rules look like the output of a simple statistical generator. Across 13 rules a model that knows only column distributions and rank correlations is off by 1.9 pp (independence: 12.0 pp). A tabular generator (copula / GAN) or heavy independent noise on each column would do exactly this; the data cannot tell which, and real extracts also break some identities (timing, FX, rounding). -> Model the columns as given; expect relationships between columns to be statistical, not accounting identities; ask the organisers how the data were produced.
- **WARNING** - 4 columns marked 'known at origination' follow the loan's delinquency state. `INTEREST_RATE_NOMINAL` (state AUC 0.76), `ECONOMIC_SECTORS` (state AUC 0.73; 0 without business: 1% of clean vs 37% of look-alike loans), `ECONOMIC_SECTORS_PURPOSE` (state AUC 0.67; 0 without business: 62% of clean vs 96% of look-alike loans), `PRINCIPAL_PAYMENT_FREQUENCY` (state AUC 0.66; 1 twice a month (unverified): 2% of clean vs 29% of look-alike loans) -> Treat them as state indicators (fields blanked or switched as a loan goes bad), not as application facts: the hand-off table lists a stricter application set (in_A_strict) without them.
- **WARNING** - Delinquency histories look templated. 10 exact values away from any column ceiling appear far too often; flagging any row with one of them catches 71% of all events at 75% precision (the values were picked on these same rows, so read it as an upper bound). A further 11 values sit at a column's ceiling, which is natural for chronic cases and is not counted here. -> A model will learn these fingerprints: great leaderboard scores here are not evidence of real predictive power
- **WARNING** - The supplied score / PD is driven by arrears information. best AUC among the supplied columns is 0.931 overall but 0.590 (±0.07) among the 63,073 loans with no delinquency history, which contain only 70 events. -> Use it as a benchmark only on the 'no delinquency history' population, never as a feature when predicting the status.
- **WARNING** - For 56% of the loans the whole transaction window comes AFTER the loan started. Only 44% of the simulated loans were granted inside the 12-month window. For the rest, every bank-data feature describes behaviour while the loan was already running (instalments, arrears, penalties), so these are monitoring / behavioural-scoring features, comparable to feature set B, and not application-time features. A genuine application scorecard would need the history BEFORE each loan's start date, which this mock does not contain. -> never mix them with set A in an application-scorecard framing
